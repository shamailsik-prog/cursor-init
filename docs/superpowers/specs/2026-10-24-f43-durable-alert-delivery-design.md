## 0. Understanding

**What the brief asks.** Logs are not a delivery mechanism of record. Every critical condition writes an idempotent Firestore alert document, in the same transaction as its state change or through a fenced step, carrying severity, owner role, firm, subject reference, first and last seen, count, a state (`open`, `acknowledged`, `resolved`) and an escalation deadline, and no restricted text. The owning role acknowledges or resolves through an authenticated, P5-authorised endpoint, with an audit trail. A scheduled escalator raises severity and re-targets the next owner when a deadline passes unacknowledged, idempotently and fenced. External delivery goes through one notifier interface: an in-repo outbox and notification document plus a structured log line that the F32 manifest's log-based policy matches. Alert policies are code. Every route is exercised by fault injection under the emulator.

**Constraint.** There is no cloud access. A page reaching a real phone or inbox is NOT DEMONSTRATED. Everything code, the emulator and CI can prove is closed in the repo.

**Assumptions (mine, each recorded as a ruling).** The named operator is the on-call address the F32 Terraform already declares (`var.oncall_email`, `google_monitoring_notification_channel.oncall`). The firm administrator is the firm's founding partner, whom `onboardFirm` records in `platform_onboarding/{uid}`. The existing `notifications` collection (per-user, readable and markable by its user in `firestore.rules`) is the in-repo notification surface; nothing writes it today.

## 2. Approaches considered

**A. Keep log lines, add more log-based policies.** Cheapest. Rejected: a log line is lost with logging, carries no state, cannot be acknowledged and cannot escalate; the finding is exactly that logs are the delivery mechanism.

**B. Raise alerts by subscribing to the existing events through the P2 engine** (fan-out a `job.deadLettered` or `ai.budget.threshold_crossed` event into an alert job). Uses the engine's exactly-once `runFenced`. Rejected as the primary path: the core failure scenario is a broken Cloud Tasks dispatch, and an alert job dispatched through that same queue would never run. The alerting path must not depend on the thing it watches. It would also touch `p2/fanout.ts` subscribers (F36's lane).

**C. (chosen) A durable alert document written where the state change is written, plus a scheduled checker for conditions that have no single transaction, plus a scheduled escalator, behind one notifier interface.**
- Where a transaction already records the condition (dead letter, budget crossing, a blocked call), the alert is read and written in that same transaction: it exists if and only if the state change committed.
- Where the condition is a duration or a repetition (a job not dispatched, a queue or saga that has waited too long, spend that stays unresolved), the condition's durable state already lives on the rows (`redispatches`, `nextAttemptAt`, `unmerge.startedAt`, `leaseUntil`), and a scheduled pass derives the alert from it idempotently. If that write fails, the next pass derives it again from the same rows.
- An escalator (every minute) delivers pending notifications through the notifier and escalates open alerts whose deadline has passed, each in a fenced transaction.

*Steelman for B:* exactly-once delivery is already solved in P2, so reusing it is less new code. *Answer:* the dead-letter alert is already exactly-once under C, because it shares the dead-letter transaction, and only C keeps alerting alive when the queue is the fault. *Devil's advocate against C:* two derivation mechanisms (in-transaction and scheduled) are more surface than one, and a derived alert can lag its condition by one schedule period. *Answer:* each condition has exactly one owner of its derivation (table in section 4); the lag is one sweep (1 minute) or one check (5 minutes) against thresholds of 15 to 60 minutes. *Bias check:* C is also the design I can prove end to end under the emulator; that matches the brief's scope ruling rather than a preference.

## 3. Rulings

**R1. The alert document is the delivery mechanism of record.** Collection `ops_alerts`, document id `"a" + sha256(JSON.stringify([condition, firmId, subjectKey]))[0:40]` (the `jobIdFor` pattern, with a JSON array instead of a NUL join: the unit test showed `("a", "b\0c")` and `("a\0b", "c")` collide under a join). One document per condition and subject. Fields: `schemaVersion`, `condition`, `firmId`, `subjectKey` (`kind:id`, e.g. `job:j3a…`, `jobType:platform.diagnostic`, `budget:2026-10:90`), `ref` (the latest concrete document path, e.g. `jobs/j3a…`, or null), `detail` (codes, ids and integers only), `baseSeverity`, `severity`, `state`, `level`, `ownerRole`, `episode`, `count` (this episode), `totalCount`, `firstSeenAt` (episode start), `lastSeenAt`, `escalateAt` (the acknowledgment deadline, or null), `escalations` (this episode's steps), `actions` (acknowledge and resolve records, last 20), `acknowledgedAt`/`acknowledgedBy`, `resolvedAt`/`resolvedBy`, `createdAt`, `updatedAt`. *Reason:* the brief's field list, with the episode and history fields that make reopening and the audit trail exact.

**R2. No restricted text.** `detail` accepts at most 12 keys (`^[A-Za-z][A-Za-z0-9_]{0,31}$`), each a safe integer or a string matching `^[A-Za-z0-9_.:/-]{1,128}$`; anything else throws before any write (a programming error, never data). The builders (`alerts/specs.ts`) pass every value through `detailText` / `detailInt` and every reference through `safeRef`, so an odd job type or model id becomes `"unknown"` instead of failing the P1 or P2 transaction that hosts the raise. Subjects and refs are document ids and paths. Job payloads, matter titles, client names, notes, prompts and error messages never enter an alert, an outbox row or a notification. *Reason:* the brief's "store references only"; an alert is read by operators who are not members of the matter.

**R3. Raise semantics (dedupe).** A raise reads the alert in the caller's transaction, then writes it:
- absent: created `open`, `level` 0, `ownerRole` `operator`, `severity` the condition's, `episode` 1, `count` 1, deadline `now + ackWindow(severity)`, and one level-0 notification staged in the outbox;
- `open` or `acknowledged`: `count + 1`, `totalCount + 1`, `lastSeenAt`, latest `ref` and `detail`; severity only rises; no notification (this is the dedupe: one page per episode and level);
- `resolved`: reopened as a new episode (`episode + 1`, `count` 1, `firstSeenAt` now, level 0, fresh deadline, acknowledgment and resolution cleared) with a new level-0 notification.
*Reason:* "created once (deduped)" and "resolve closes the alert" both hold, and a condition that recurs after a human closed it is never silent.

**R4. Severity, deadlines and the owner ladder.** Severities `warning` < `high` < `critical`. Acknowledgment windows: critical 15 minutes, high 30 minutes, warning 4 hours. The ladder is `operator` (level 0) → `partner` (level 1) → `firm_admin` (level 2). *Why this order:* every condition in section 4 is first an operational fault (a queue, a store, the reconciler, a provider budget) that only the platform operator can repair, so the operator is paged first; if the operator does not acknowledge, the firm whose work is stalled is told next, through its partners, who can retry or cancel jobs, raise a budget or call the operator; if no partner acknowledges, the firm administrator, the person accountable for the firm's account, is told as critical. *Firm administrator:* the founding partner recorded by `onboardFirm` in `platform_onboarding/{uid}` (FN-1), if that user is still an active partner of the firm; otherwise every active partner, marked `fallback: "partners"` on the notification. There is no separate admin role in this codebase (firm administration is partner-only in the shared decision table), so this is the only durable record of who administers a firm.

**R5. Escalation is fenced and exactly once per step.** The escalator queries `state == "open" and escalateAt <= now` (oldest first) and, per alert, runs a transaction that re-reads it and acts only if it is still `open`, still at the level the query saw, and still past its deadline. It then sets `level + 1`, the next owner, severity one step higher (capped at critical), a new deadline `now + ackWindow(new severity)` (none after the last level), appends the step to `escalations`, and stages the notification for the new owner. A second escalator run, a concurrent run, or a run long after the deadline cannot take two steps at once: the new deadline is computed from now. The function runs with `maxInstances: 1` as well. *Reason:* "a missed deadline escalates exactly once per step"; the fence is the re-read compare, the same pattern as the sweeper's per-row re-read.

**R6. Acknowledgment and resolution (the owning role).** Four routes under `/api/platform/alerts` on the `api` router (U-1: it is the only entry point to P5 operations, so no new `onRequest`):
- `GET listAlerts` (firm; `alert.read`; optional `state`; paginated with `limit` and `cursor`): the partner worklist, newest `lastSeenAt` first, the firm's alerts only.
- `POST acknowledgeAlert {alertId}` and `POST resolveAlert {alertId}` (firm; `alert.ack`).
- `POST operatorAlertAction {alertId, action: "acknowledge" | "resolve"}` (firmless; `platformAdmin` claim, like `setPlatformAiKillSwitch`).
`alert.read` and `alert.ack` are new rows of the shared P5 table, partner only, staff only, accounts refused (the table's existing order). *Why a new table row:* an action the table does not know falls through to `allow ok` for every staff role, intern included; the row makes the routes fail closed. Owner rule: an alert at level *k* may be acted on by the owners of levels 0 to *k*: the operator always; a partner from level 1 (the partner owns it once it has escalated to the firm; at level 0 it is the operator's, and a partner gets 403 `alert_not_owner`). Another firm's alert, or none, is 404 `not_found` (no existence oracle). Acknowledge needs `open` (else 409 `alert_already_acknowledged` or `alert_resolved`); resolve needs `open` or `acknowledged` (else 409 `alert_resolved`). The transition, the `actions` entry and the call's audit row (`audit.stage`, which also stages the idempotency effect marker) commit in one transaction. No note is taken: a free-text note would put staff prose into the alert (R2); the audit trail is the actor, role, level and time. The operator's audit row is written to the alert's firm, so that firm's partners can see who acted. The router gives a firmless route no clock, so the operator route uses `Date.now()`, replaceable in tests through `alertOpsTestSeam` (the `jobRuntime` / `sweepTestSeam` pattern; never set in production code).

**R7. One notifier interface.** `AlertNotifier.deliver(notification)`. Each owner change stages one outbox row `ops_alert_notifications/{alertId}_e{episode}_l{level}` (deterministic id, so a transaction retry rewrites the same row) with the targets: always `{kind: "oncall"}`, plus `{kind: "user", uid}` for the partners (level 1) or the firm administrator (level 2). For each user target the same transaction writes `notifications/{outboxId}_{uid}` (`firmId`, `userId`, `kind: "ops_alert"`, `alertId`, `condition`, `severity`, `level`, `read: false`, `readAt: null`), which the existing rule lets that user read and mark read. The escalator's delivery pass claims each `pending` outbox row created by now (a transaction that re-reads it and sets `status: "sending"` with a 2-minute claim), hands it to the notifier, then marks it `delivered`; a notifier failure puts it back to `pending` for the next run, and a run that dies after its claim leaves it to be re-claimed once the claim lapses. So concurrent runs hand a page over once, and delivery is at least once. The production notifier writes `logger.error("ops_alert.notified", { pageId, alertId, firmId, condition, severity, level, ownerRole, episode, users, fallback })`: the F32 manifest registers it, the rendered Terraform matches it and notifies `google_monitoring_notification_channel.oncall`. *Reason:* a duplicate page is safe, a lost one is the finding. *Reason:* a duplicate page is safe, a lost one is the finding. Real email, SMS or pager delivery is NOT DEMONSTRATED.

**R8. Conditions, their owners of derivation and thresholds** (section 4 has the full catalogue):
- `job.dead_lettered` (high, per job): in the dead-letter transaction (worker claim at attempt 4, worker commit, sweeper query B).
- `job.dispatch_stalled` (high, per firm and job type): the sweeper. A re-dispatch that brings a row's `redispatches` to 3 or more, or that finds the row due for 15 minutes or more, is recorded; after the sweep each firm and type gets one raise. Three sweeper re-dispatches without a claim take about 3.5 minutes (30 + 60 + 120 s of backoff) and mean the queue is not delivering. *Why after the sweep and not in each row's transaction:* a broken queue stalls every job; one alert document written by hundreds of row transactions would be a hot document (about one write a second sustained) and would make the sweeper's own transactions fail during the very incident. The rows' `redispatches` and `nextAttemptAt` are the durable state; a failed raise is logged `ops_alert.raise_failed` at ERROR and derived again at the row's next re-dispatch (at most 600 s later), and the checker's `job.queue_age` covers a sweeper that is not running at all.
- `job.commit_failed` (high, per firm and job type): the worker, after a commit transaction fails, in a transaction of its own bounded to 5 s. The commit failed, so there is no transaction to share; the durable backstop is `job.queue_age` (the row stays `running` past its lease) and, at attempt 4, `job.dead_lettered`.
- `job.queue_age` (high, per firm and job type): the checker. Oldest `queued` or `failed` engine row due 15 minutes ago or more, and `running` rows whose lease expired 15 minutes ago or more.
- `saga.age` (high, per firm and saga type `entity.unmerge`): the checker. `merge_events` whose `unmerge.status` is `in_progress` and whose `unmerge.startedAt` is 60 minutes old or more. A healthy un-merge finishes in seconds to minutes.
- `ai.budget_threshold` (70: warning, 90: high; per firm, month and threshold): in the P1 commit transaction that creates `ai_budget_alerts/...`.
- `ai.kill_switch_blocked` (warning; per firm and switch scope `platform` or `firm`): in the commit transaction of a call blocked by a kill switch.
- `ai.reservation_failed` (high; per firm): in the commit transaction of a call blocked `reservation_failed` (the reservation or the attempt record failed).
- `ai.reservation_unresolved` (high; per firm): the checker. Reservations still `reserved` 10 minutes or more after their lease (the reconciler runs every minute), and every `reconcile_failed` (quarantined) reservation. This is also the durable form of `p1.usage_log_failed`, whose reservation stays held.
*Reason:* thresholds are judgements recorded here, each well above its mechanism's normal latency and well below "indefinitely"; they are constants in `alerts/model.ts`, unit-pinned. The sweeper raises at most 20 stall groups per sweep, within 5 s past its 30 s budget (each raise bounded to 5 s), so the sweep still ends inside its 45 s timeout; groups not reached are raised by a later sweep.

**R9. Transaction coupling in P1 and P2.** Firestore transactions read before they write. In the worker and the sweeper the dead-letter decision is known right after the row is read and before anything is written, so the alert is read there (`willRetry`, shared with `stageFailureRow` so the two cannot disagree). In `p1/gateway.ts` `stage`, the alert reads follow the existing reads and precede the first write; `CommitArgs` gains an optional `nowMs` (the call's clock) for the alert times, defaulting to `Date.now()` (the reconciler's own `stage` call is unchanged). Gateway edits are limited to these lines because F34 edits the same file.

**R10. The checker and the escalator are two schedules.** `alertChecker` every 5 minutes (timeout 60 s, budget 40 s, each query read oldest first in pages of 100, at most 5 pages per query per run, from a persisted cursor: R10a); `alertEscalator` every minute (timeout 45 s, budget 30 s; escalation first, then delivery, so an escalation's page goes out in the same run). Both `maxInstances: 1`, internal ingress, the runtime account. Each pass is caught on its own and logged `ops_alert.pass_failed` (ERROR, registered), so a missing index on one query does not stop the others. *Reason:* escalation needs minute granularity; scanning queues every minute would read up to 2,000 rows a minute for thresholds of 15 minutes and more.

**R10a. The checker pages through a backlog across runs (PR #120 review, Codex P2).** A fixed first 500 rows per query, with no cursor, would bring back the same prefix every run during a persistent backlog and never report what lies behind it (another firm's incident among them). Each of the five queries (waiting jobs, running jobs past their lease, sagas, held reservations, quarantined reservations) is ordered by its time field and the document id (the quarantined query by the id alone) and read in pages of 100, at most 5 pages per query per run, from a cursor kept in `platform/opsAlertChecker` (server-only through the rules' catch-all deny, like `platform/p1ReconcileCursor`). A short page means the end: the cursor is cleared and the next run starts again from the oldest row. Every row of a backlog is therefore read within ceil(backlog / 500) runs. An alert's counts are of the rows that run read. *Reason:* bounded reads per run (the same 500-row budget as before) with guaranteed progress; the reconciler's sweep (Pass C) uses the same cursor-and-wrap pattern.

**R11. Alert policies as code.** `infra/deploy-manifest.json` registers `ops_alert.notified`, `ops_alert.raise_failed` and `ops_alert.pass_failed` (ERROR, runbook `functions/src/alerts/README.md`) and declares `alertChecker` and `alertEscalator`; `infra/terraform/alerts.tf` is re-rendered (`infra/render_terraform.py --write`); the F32 gate stays green. The existing lines (`job.deadLettered`, the P1 ERROR lines) keep their policies: the durable alert is added, not substituted.

**R11a. The filters match the runtime that writes the logs (PR #120 review, Codex P1).** The renderer (`infra/render_terraform.py`, from F32) filtered every condition on `resource.type="cloud_function"`, the first-generation resource type. Every export here is second generation (`firebase-functions/v2/*` triggers; each `__endpoint.platform` is `gcfv2`; the root `firebase-functions` import is used only for `logger`), and second-generation runtime logs carry `resource.type="cloud_run_revision"`, so no policy could ever match, F32's existing ones included. Ruling: `cloud_run_revision` alone; `(cloud_run_revision OR cloud_function)` only if an export were first generation, which none is. The change is the renderer's filter line, with `infra/terraform/alerts.tf` re-rendered. `functions/test/deploy-contract-alert-runtime.test.ts` derives the expected resource types from every export's `__endpoint.platform` (any function may emit any registered line: the emitting modules, `log.ts` among them, are shared) and checks both the renderer's output and the committed file. This also repairs F32's existing alert policies.

**R12. Rules and indexes.** `ops_alerts` and `ops_alert_notifications` are server-only (`allow read, write: if false`): the worklist is read through `listAlerts`, which applies P5, so no client read rule is needed. Composite indexes: `jobs (status, nextAttemptAt)`, `merge_events (unmerge.status, unmerge.startedAt)`, `ops_alerts (state, escalateAt)`, `ops_alerts (firmId, lastSeenAt desc, __name__ desc)`, `ops_alerts (firmId, state, lastSeenAt desc, __name__ desc)`, `ops_alert_notifications (status, createdAt)`. No TTL: alerts are records of who was told and who acted (retention belongs to F42); no new `expiresAt` field, so the F32 TTL checks are unaffected.

**R13. Corpus source freshness is a follow-up.** It is not in Firestore. The exact hook: `corpus_service/scraper/tasks/job_record.py` `_alerts` (:291) and the two `logger.error("F28 source alert: ...")` sites (:125, :287) should call an alert writer for `corpus.source_stale` / `corpus.source_failing` (per source), either into this collection through a Functions endpoint the corpus service can reach or into a Postgres table the checker reads. What stays log-only, and why.** The remaining ERROR and WARN lines in section 4 are either already registered in the manifest and covered by a durable alert above (for example `p1.usage_log_failed`, durable as `ai.reservation_unresolved`), or are diagnostics without a human action (`job.lease_lost`, `event.fanout_ignored`, `*_budget_spent`). `autoconfirm.backlog_stale` (F38) and the audit-outbox lines (F11) already have durable state of their own (`backlog.alert`, `audit_dead_letter`) and registered policies; turning them into `ops_alerts` is F43-FU2.

## 5. Flows

**Dead letter.** Worker or sweeper transaction: read job → (dead letter) read alert → write job, events, audit, alert, outbox L0 → commit. After commit the existing `job.deadLettered` log line. The next escalator run delivers the L0 page.

**Broken dispatch.** Enqueue → dispatch throws → sweeper re-dispatch 1, 2, 3 (each stamps `redispatches`) → at the third, the sweep's stall set holds the row → one raise for (firm, job type) → L0 page. If the sweeper is down too: the checker raises `job.queue_age` at 15 minutes.

**Escalation.** L0 deadline passes → escalator: L1, severity +1, `notifications` for each active partner, outbox L1 → L1 deadline passes → L2, critical, the founding partner (or the partners), outbox L2 → no further step. Acknowledge at any point clears `escalateAt`; the escalator's query no longer returns it.

### 8.2 Rulings

**R15. The filter matches a structured `event` field, not the message (R1-001a; option ii).** Every registered ERROR line carries `event: "<name>"` in its structured data, as the last property of the object literal (after any spread, so a spread payload can never override it), and each policy filters `resource.type="cloud_run_revision" AND severity="ERROR" AND jsonPayload.event="<name>"`. *Why:* `entryFromArgs` spreads the structured data into the entry and then sets only `severity` and `message`, so `jsonPayload.event` survives the stack-trace rewrite, an `Error` argument, and the agent's text-payload rule; equality is exact (case ignored), so `p1.reconcile_failed` cannot match `p1.reconcile_failed_x`. *Steelman for option (i), a regex `jsonPayload.message=~"^Error: <event>(\\n|$)"`:* no emitter changes, one renderer line. *Answer:* it binds every page to two undocumented details of one library version (the `Error: ` prefix and the stack layout) and breaks for any call that passes an `Error` (then the message is `util.format` of both, and no rewrite happens). *Devil's advocate against (ii):* an emitter added later without the field pages no one, the same silent failure. *Answer:* `functions/test/deploy-contract-alert-runtime.test.ts` walks the registry over the TypeScript AST of `functions/src` and fails for any `.error("<registered event>", …)` whose data does not end with that `event`, and for any ERROR line whose `event` names another event; G-0 [20] still refuses an unregistered ERROR event. The omission is a red build, not a quiet gap. The WARN twin of `audit.outbox_reconcile_failed` is left as it is (the policy's `severity="ERROR"` never matches it).

**R16. One policy per event, correctly quoted, rate-limited (R1-001b, c, d).** `infra/render_terraform.py` renders one `google_monitoring_alert_policy` per registered `functions` event, named `log_<event, each non-identifier character as _>` (a collision refuses to render), with: `combiner = "OR"`; exactly one `conditions { display_name; condition_matched_log { filter } }`; `alert_strategy { notification_rate_limit { period = "300s" } auto_close = "1800s" }`; `notification_channels = [google_monitoring_notification_channel.oncall.name]`; and `documentation { content = "Runbook: <path>" mime_type = "text/markdown" }` (the page names its runbook). Every string goes through one HCL quoting function: double quotes, `\` `"` and control characters escaped, `${` and `%{` doubled. An event name must match `^[A-Za-z][A-Za-z0-9_]*(\.[A-Za-z0-9_]+)+$` or the render fails. **R16a.** The page policy (`ops_alert.notified`) also extracts `page_id = EXTRACT(jsonPayload.pageId)`: Terraform documents each combination of extracted labels as a separate rule for notifications, so the 300 s rate limit applies per page, and two different alerts paged in the same five minutes are two notifications instead of one (the same page delivered twice, at least once, is still one). The manifest carries this as `labels` on that policy. *Why 300 s:* the escalator runs every minute; the rate limit only collapses repeats of one rule, and the durable record is in Firestore either way. *Consumer impact:* a `terraform apply` destroys `functions_errors` and creates one policy per event; nothing is deployed today.

**R17. What is proved in the repo, and what is not.** There is no `terraform` binary and no HCL library here (python-hcl2 is not installed and is not installed for this). The test therefore parses the rendered and the committed `alerts.tf` with a parser for the HCL subset the renderer emits (blocks, attributes, double-quoted strings with HCL escapes, references, lists and maps; a single quote, a heredoc, an interpolation or a comment is a parse error), checks the structure (one condition per policy, a rate limit and auto-close on every policy, the on-call channel), decodes each filter, and evaluates it with an evaluator for the subset of the Logging query language the filters use (`AND` of `field = "string"`; `=` ignores case after NFKC normalisation; a missing field is false; any other syntax throws). It evaluates against the lines that firebase-functions 6.6.0's real `logger.error` writes (captured in process at its `UNPATCHED_CONSOLE`, the same module instance the code uses), turned into a `LogEntry` by the agent's special-field rules: every filter matches its own event's line and no other, not the same event at WARN, not a line without the field. Real emitters (the notifier, the dead-letter line, the deploy contract) are run and evaluated too. `terraform validate`, `terraform fmt`, a deployed policy and a delivered email are NOT DEMONSTRATED. `render_terraform.py` also checks its own output (no single-quoted string, one condition and one rate limit per alert policy) in `--check`, so G-0 [20] refuses a regression.

**R18. An acknowledgment holds for four hours (R1-002).** `ACK_HOLD_MS = 4 h` (`alerts/model.ts`, unit-pinned). A raise of an `acknowledged` alert whose `acknowledgedAt` is `ACK_HOLD_MS` or more before the raise is treated like a raise of a `resolved` one: a new episode at level 0, with its deadline and a level-0 page (`…_e{n+1}_l0`). Within the hold it is counted, as before. An acknowledged row without a readable `acknowledgedAt` counts as past the hold (a page is the safe error). *Why four hours:* it is the longest acknowledgment window (warning); an operator who acknowledged and is working the incident is not re-paged by its own continuing symptoms for that long, and a recurrence after it is treated as a new incident. *Steelman for one hour:* faster re-paging of a new incident. *Answer:* the checker re-derives a persistent condition every five minutes, so a one-hour hold re-pages an operator mid-repair every hour; four hours is the bound on silence the brief proposed. The exception is R23.

**R19. Tests for correct code (R1-003).** PROBE-06 (a raise racing the escalator), 08 (every refused role, the shared table), 09 (the ladder's targets and the founder fallback) go into the escalation file; 14 and 15 (the stall rule's two arms, a queue that accepts and never delivers) into the P2 file. New paging tests: 150 rows sharing one timestamp across a page edge (C2: without the id tiebreak the rows after the edge are skipped), and a budget that runs out after the first page (C4: the cursor must keep its place, not wrap). *Result:* C2 showed no defect (the test passes on main). C4's cursor kept its place on main (no C4 defect), but the test found a different one: the run that stopped on its budget dropped the groups it had read, because the raise loop stopped on the same spent budget while the saved cursor had already moved past those rows, so they waited for the wrap. Fixed by the raise grace of R20; `scan` is unchanged.

**R20. Paging order, rotation and the bound (R1-004).** All five checker queries are now time-ordered: the quarantined reservations by `quarantinedAt` (set with `state: "reconcile_failed"` by the reconciler since that state exists, 62d8e41), then the id; new composite index `ai_reservations (state, quarantinedAt)`. A stored cursor whose time value is not a Timestamp is ignored (the query restarts from its oldest row), which retires the old id-only cursor. The starting pass rotates: `platform/opsAlertChecker.nextPass` names the pass that starts the next run, saved at the start of each run. A pass's raises may use `CHECK_RAISE_GRACE_MS` (5 s) past the scan budget, as the sweeper's stall raises do, so the groups a run read are not dropped when its scan used the budget (their rows are behind the saved cursor). *The bound, stated exactly:* in runs where a pass reads its full window of W = 500 rows per query, a row present when the query's cycle begins is read within ceil(N/W) runs, and a row that enters behind the cursor within 2·ceil(N/W) − 1 runs (the rest of the cycle, then its place in the next); with every query time-ordered a newly eligible row usually enters ahead of the cursor. When the budget, not the window, ends a run, every pass still starts first at least once in three runs and reads at least one page of 100 then, so the degenerate bound is 3·(2·ceil(N/100) − 1) runs.

**R21. The last level re-pages until someone acknowledges (R1-005).** At level 2 the alert keeps a deadline of one critical window (15 minutes); when it passes, the escalator re-pages level 2 (the firm administrator, or the partners as fallback, and the on-call channel) as page `…_e{n}_l2_r{k}`, counts `repages`, and sets the next deadline from now, fenced like a step (still open, still level 2, still past the deadline). It stops when the alert is acknowledged, resolved or cleared (R22). Re-pages are reported as `repaged`, not `escalated`, and are not appended to `escalations` (which would grow without bound). *Second channel:* not added. An `escalation_email` variable is a deploy-surface change to the F32 Terraform the brief makes optional; the on-call channel already receives every page of every level. Recorded as F43-FU6.

**R22. A derived condition that is no longer observed is cleared (R1-006a).** After a checker pass reads all of its queries completely in one run (each started from no cursor and reached its end), every `open` alert of that pass's condition (`job.queue_age`, `saga.age`, `ai.reservation_unresolved`) whose firm and subject the pass did not observe gets `clearedAt` and loses its deadline (`escalateAt: null`), in a transaction that re-reads it; so the escalator, which only steps alerts with a deadline, skips it. The state stays `open` (a person still resolves it). A run that read only part of a query clears nothing. If the condition is observed again, the raise counts it, removes `clearedAt` and restores a deadline at its current level. *Why only these three:* they are states the checker can observe end to end; a dead letter, a dispatch stall, a failed commit or a budget crossing is an event, not a state.

**R23. Kill-switch alerts stay at level 0 (R1-006b).** A kill switch is only ever set by a deliberate action (`setFirmAiKillSwitch` by a partner, `setPlatformAiKillSwitch` by a platform admin), so `ai.kill_switch_blocked` is created without a deadline: the on-call operator is paged once at level 0, the partners see it on their worklist, and nobody is escalated to about a switch they set. For the same reason an acknowledged kill-switch alert is not reopened by the hold of R18 (the switch is one standing decision, not recurring faults); resolving it while the switch is still on reopens it at the next blocked call, as before.

**R24. A platform kill switch is one alert (R1-006c).** A call refused by the platform switch raises `ai.kill_switch_blocked` under the platform subject (`firmId: "__platform__"`, subject `killSwitch:platform`, ref `platform/p1`), not under the caller's firm. `__platform__` can never be a firm (Firestore reserves document ids matching `__.*__`), so no partner route, worklist or ladder reaches it; the operator acts on it with `operatorAlertAction`. Because every blocked call in every firm would otherwise write that one document in its commit transaction (a hot document during exactly the incident), the spec is `observeOnly`: a raise writes the alert only to create or reopen it, and an existing live alert is read and left as it is (no count).

**R25. The G-2 route probe gets a 60 s timeout (R1-007).** One `{ timeout: 60_000 }` on that `it` in `functions/test/g2.cross-firm.test.ts`; nothing is skipped or weakened. Measured before and after (the plan's ledger).

**R26. The embedding path's alerts use the call's clock (R1-008).** `callP1Embedding`'s commit passes `nowMs: Math.floor(now())`, the one change in `embedding.ts` apart from R15's `event` fields. The test drives a kill-switch-blocked corpus embedding and a 70 percent crossing through `callP1Embedding` and checks the alerts and their times.

Events are registered only through `infra/deploy-manifest.json`; whichever of this branch and the F34 follow-up lands second re-renders after merging main, and an event added there (`p1.opik_misconfigured`) needs `event` on its emitter, which the registry walk enforces. *What happened:* the F34 follow-up landed first (#129, 6b186a0); this branch merged main, re-rendered (25 policies), and added `event` to `functions/src/p1/opik.ts`'s line and to the two F34 tests that pin its payload.
